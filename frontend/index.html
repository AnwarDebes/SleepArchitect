How SleepArchitect works

Sleep researchers have a 40-year-old quantitative model -- Borbely's two-process model -- that predicts sleep timing from just two variables: Process S (homeostatic sleep pressure, rising linearly during wake and falling exponentially during sleep) and Process C (circadian propensity, a sinusoid set by your body clock). Adding caffeine pharmacokinetics, alcohol pharmacokinetics, exercise effects, meal-timing effects, and light-PRC-based circadian phase shifts gives a complete personalised sleep predictor.

Existing sleep apps (Oura, Whoop, Apple Health, Sleep Cycle, AutoSleep) track sleep retrospectively. SleepArchitect is, to our knowledge, the first free open-source tool that predicts tonight's sleep from today's caffeine, alcohol, exercise, light, and meal timing.

Privacy

SleepArchitect runs entirely in your browser. No analytics, no cookies, no network calls. Profile is stored only in your browser's localStorage.

Limitations

This is a forecast not a sleep study. Sleep architecture predictions are population averages, not personalised polysomnography. For clinical sleep concerns (apnoea, insomnia, RLS, narcolepsy), see a sleep physician. Inputs assume non-pregnant healthy adults; use pregnancy and CYP1A2 toggles for relevant covariates.

SleepArchitect -- public domain (CC0). Borbely-style two-process model with sleep-modulator coupling.
